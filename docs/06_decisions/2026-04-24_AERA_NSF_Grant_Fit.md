# AERA-NSF Grants Program Fit Note

## Opportunity

- Program: AERA-NSF Grants Program, Dissertation Grants and Research Grants
- Posted deadline in current announcement: 2026-05-20
- Dissertation Grant amount in current announcement: up to $27,500
- Research Grant amount in current announcement: up to $35,000
- Program contact: `grantsprogram@aera.net`
- Application portal: `https://apply.aera.net/`

Note: the public AERA Apply portal confirms that the AERA-NSF Grants Program has an active call, but public key-date pages may lag the newest announcement. Confirm the 2026-05-20 deadline in the MyAERA/AERA Apply application record before submission.

## Fit Assessment

The current AI Adoption in Education MASEM project is a strong education research project, but it is not automatically a strong AERA-NSF Grants Program proposal in its present form.

The main reason is program fit: the AERA-NSF Grants Program prioritizes advanced quantitative analysis of large-scale, federally supported or federally enhanced datasets. The current MASEM dataset is a literature-derived meta-analytic dataset assembled from published studies. That is rigorous, but it is not itself an NCES, NSF, federal administrative, statewide longitudinal, or major federally supported international dataset.

## Recommendation

Apply only if the proposal is reframed around a federally supported large-scale dataset or a federally enhanced data system.

Do not submit the current Paper A or Paper B project as-is. A direct proposal framed as "AI adoption meta-analysis in higher education" would likely be vulnerable to a desk-fit or review-fit objection because the analytic dataset is not the kind of large-scale dataset the program is designed to support.

## Viable Reframing Options

### Option 1: International Large-Scale Assessment Link

Frame the project around students' AI/digital technology readiness using an eligible international or federally supported large-scale dataset, then use the MASEM as the theory-building and construct-mapping foundation.

Possible direction:

> How do digital learning conditions, technology self-efficacy, school support, and student attitudes predict readiness for AI-supported learning across education systems?

The MASEM contributes the theoretical model; the grant analysis uses the eligible large-scale dataset.

### Option 2: NCES Higher Education Data Link

Frame the project around institutional capacity for AI adoption in U.S. higher education using NCES or other federally supported higher education datasets, with the MASEM as the conceptual map of individual adoption mechanisms.

Possible direction:

> How do institutional resources, student population characteristics, and technology infrastructure shape readiness for AI-supported instruction in U.S. postsecondary education?

This is more policy-facing, but may be farther from the current construct-level MASEM.

### Option 3: Methodological Grant

Frame the proposal as a measurement/methods study that links meta-analytic construct harmonization with large-scale secondary data measures.

Possible direction:

> Can AI adoption constructs derived from MASEM literature be operationalized and validated in large-scale education datasets?

This aligns with the project's distinctive strength: construct harmonization and MASEM-ready measurement logic.

## Best Current Strategy

The strongest version is Option 3.

The proposal should not lead with "we are doing a meta-analysis." It should lead with the problem that large-scale education datasets increasingly contain technology and digital learning measures, but the constructs used to interpret AI adoption are fragmented across TAM, UTAUT, trust/risk, anxiety, and attitude traditions. The project can use the MASEM evidence base to specify a theory-informed measurement framework, then test or map that framework in an eligible large-scale dataset.

## Preliminary Working Title

> Measuring Readiness for AI-Supported Learning: Linking Meta-Analytic Adoption Theory With Large-Scale Education Data

## What Must Be Solved Before Applying

1. Identify the eligible dataset.
2. Confirm that the dataset is federally supported, federally enhanced, or otherwise eligible under the AERA-NSF call.
3. Map MASEM constructs to available variables.
4. Decide whether the applicant category is Dissertation Grant or Research Grant.
5. Build a concise four-page proposal around advanced quantitative analysis, not around literature review alone.

## Risks

- The current MASEM dataset alone is likely insufficient for this grant.
- The project needs explicit U.S. education policy or practice relevance if using international data.
- The deadline is close; the proposal needs a narrow, dataset-ready question.

## Bottom Line

This is worth pursuing if it becomes a dataset-linked quantitative proposal. It is not worth submitting as a standalone Paper A/Paper B meta-analysis proposal.
